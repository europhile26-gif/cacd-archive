Privacy

Last updated: 10 September 2026

The short version

This site sets no cookies for tracking and stores nothing in your browser for analytics. There is no third-party analytics service, no advertising network and no tracking pixel.

The server keeps a basic record of requests — including your IP address — for up to 30 days, so we can see how the site is used and spot abuse. We never record what you searched for.

Who we are

CACD Archive publishes an archive of Court of Appeal (Criminal Division) daily cause lists. For UK data protection law, the operator of this site is the data controller. To contact us about anything on this page, including a request relating to your own data, use the contact route given at the bottom of this page.

What we collect

Server request records

Like almost any web server, this site records basic technical details of each request. These records are generated on the server. Nothing is stored on your device — no cookies, no local storage, and no analytics script runs in your browser.

What Why
IP address Identifying abuse and automated scraping; approximate location and network
Date and time of the request Understanding traffic patterns and enforcing our retention period
Request method and page or endpoint address Understanding which parts of the site are used
Response status and how long the request took Monitoring errors and performance
Browser user agent string Distinguishing browsers from automated crawlers
Country and network operator (ASN), derived from the IP address Distinguishing real visitors from datacentre, VPN and crawler traffic, and applying country-level blocking where necessary
A short-lived pseudonymous identifier (see below) Counting visitors rather than raw requests
Your account identifier, if you are signed in Diagnosing problems reported by signed-in users

What we deliberately do not collect

  • Search terms and case numbers. When you search this archive, the terms you use are never written to our request records. We store only the address of the endpoint that was called, never the query attached to it.
  • Your town or city — we resolve country only, not finer location.
  • The page you arrived from — no referrer is recorded.
  • Requests for images, stylesheets, fonts and scripts — only pages and API endpoints are recorded.

The pseudonymous identifier

To count visitors rather than raw requests without using a cookie, the server computes a one-way cryptographic hash from your IP address, your browser's user agent string, a secret known only to the server, and the current date. The result cannot practically be reversed to recover your IP address, and because the date forms part of it, it changes every day. This means we can tell that several requests came from the same visitor within a single day, but we cannot recognise you across days, and we cannot link you to any other website.

Account information

If you create an account, we store your name, email address and a securely hashed password, together with any saved searches you create and your notification preferences. This information is kept for as long as your account exists, and is used to operate the service and send the alerts you have asked for. It is separate from the request records described above.

Our lawful basis

For the server request records, our lawful basis is legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f) UK GDPR). The interests we pursue are understanding how the archive is used, keeping it available and performant, and protecting it from abuse and automated scraping. We have assessed that these interests are not overridden by your rights, in particular because we minimise what is collected — no search terms, no city-level location, no cross-day tracking — and delete everything within 30 days.

For account information and email alerts, our lawful basis is performance of a contract (Article 6(1)(b)) — providing the service you signed up for.

No consent banner is shown because we do not store or read any information on your device beyond what is strictly necessary to operate the site. The Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations require consent for storing information on your device; we do not do that for analytics.

How long we keep it

  • Server request records: 30 days. A scheduled job deletes anything older, automatically, every night.
  • Account information: for as long as your account exists, and deleted when you close it.

Who we share it with

We do not sell your data, and we do not share it with advertisers or analytics companies. Request records stay on our own server. Country and network lookups are performed locally, against a database held on our server — your IP address is not sent to a third party to do this. We may disclose information where we are legally required to, or where it is necessary to investigate abuse of the service.

Your rights

You have the right to:

  • ask what personal data we hold about you, and get a copy of it;
  • ask us to correct information that is wrong;
  • ask us to delete information;
  • ask us to restrict how we use it;
  • object to our use of legitimate interests — including our request records — at any time.

A practical note on making a request about the server request records: those records contain no name and no account reference unless you were signed in, so to find them we would need the IP address you were using and the approximate date and time. Without those we may be unable to identify any data relating to you — and in that case we are not required to obtain more information about you simply to find it. Records older than 30 days will already have been deleted.

You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office, the UK's data protection regulator.

Do Not Track

If your browser sends a DNT: 1 header, we do not record the request at all.

Source data

The hearing data published on this site is public information from HM Courts & Tribunals Service, licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0. It is not personal data that we have collected about you, and this notice does not govern it. If you believe a court record published here is inaccurate, that is a matter for the publishing court rather than for us.

Changes to this notice

If we change what we collect or why, we will update this page and the date at the top of it.

Contact

To exercise any of the rights above, or to ask a question about this notice, contact the site operator. If you have an account, the quickest route is to reply to any notification email you have received from us.
